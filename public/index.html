My work revolves around multiple things: environment design, optimization, world models, interpretability, bioml, and reinforcement learning. Pharmacokinetic SSMs for Haemodynamic Collapse Prediction

Rithin Nagaraj, Sudiksha Chindula, Bhaskarjyoti Das

AIiH 2026 · ★ Best Paper Nominee (top 3%)

doiarxiv
details
  • Architected a Mamba-based selective state space model to forecast intraoperative hypotension 5 to 15 minutes in advance, achieving 0.7360 AUROC and a 2.73-fold AUPRC lift over random guessing.
  • Engineered Propofol and Remifentanil effect-site concentration trajectories as explicit inputs, preventing a 13.9% AUPRC drop compared with haemodynamics-only baselines.

CAP-Adam: Inspectable Protection for Continual Learning

Rithin Nagaraj, Paras Chopra

Under review at TMLR

details
  • Proposed a continual learning optimizer that modulates parameter updates by their importance to previously learned tasks, outperforming AdamW and established baselines including Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC).
  • Achieved approximately 90% retention across 40 unique tasks while maintaining 91% mean current-task accuracy over 200 sequential tasks on Permuted MNIST with an MLP-300 model.
  • Showed that optimizer hyperparameters systematically control the stability–plasticity trade-off, enabling controlled studies of knowledge retention and adaptation.

Lossfunk

code
details

Confounding in Learned World Models for Anesthesia Dosing

PES University

code
details
  • Clinicians adjust doses in response to what they see, so a model trained on recorded surgeries can pick up those reactions instead of how the drugs actually work. Testing whether world models fall into this trap, since RL dosing policies would be trained inside them.
  • Trained 25 models (GRU, Transformer, RSSM, a 5-RSSM ensemble, and a hybrid where doses act only through pharmacokinetic–pharmacodynamic equations; 5 seeds each). The black boxes predict well: 6.9–7.2 five-minute BIS error, 27–29% better than pharmacology alone.
  • Early counterfactual tests suggest they learned only part of the story: drug effects point the right way but are just 12–21% as strong as pharmacology predicts and arrive 3–7 min late. The hybrid recovers ~72% of the expected effect with correct timing, at a 12% cost in BIS accuracy.
